# 6. Drawings on the GPU: texture arrays for bitmaps, segment buffers expanded to pen ribbons for vectors

Date: 2026-10-03

## Status

Accepted. The vector half is backed by the spike in `spikes/vector-lines/` (RESULTS.md). Amended in part by [0019](0019-pen-lines-as-sampled-quads.md) (proposed) for pen lines such as the dust hatching: v21's overlap quads, unioned per sample, in place of capsule coverage.

## Context

The library is 469 source drawings, cut into two kinds of mark:

- **Bitmap sheets** (`assets/drawings/bitmap/`): grids of square cells with ink in alpha. They are dots (500 cells of 32 px), knots (160 × 48), stars (48 × 64), cores (27 × 128), fgstars (29 × 96), pieces (638 × 32), and strokes (60 rows of 512 × 64, tiled along a curve). The reference uploads each sheet as one 2D texture and mipmaps it whole (app23.js:L1100–1107). The cells have no padding, so at small sizes a cell's mips mix with its neighbours'. Dots are drawn about 3 px across from 32 px cells, so they use mip levels 3–4, where each cell is 2–4 texels. The ink look comes from a smoothstep on the sampled alpha (`uEdge` 0.12–0.55, L1092, L1124).
- **Vector records** (`assets/drawings/vector/`, 429 records): polylines (`l`), dots (`d`) and blobs (`b`) in a unit tile. Records average 22–75 segments, with a maximum of 267. The reference expands each line segment into a quad of half-width `PEN.line/2` _in plate pixels_, extended by 0.9 of the width at each end so joins close (`expandVector`, L1190–1213). Pen weight is therefore the same however large the drawing is placed. Drawings are also _non-affinely warped_: the spiral rewind (L842), tidal warps carrying merging galaxies (L1248, L1195), and lens Jacobians per image (L660).

The options considered for vectors were:

- **(V1) segment buffers expanded to ribbons on the GPU**, with vertex pulling and per-vertex warps;
- **(V2) signed or unsigned distance-field textures**, one per drawing, thresholded at the pen half-width;
- **(V3) both**, with SDF as a level of detail for tiny instances.

For bitmaps:

- **(B1)** keep the sheets as 2D textures with a padded layout;
- **(B2)** texture arrays, one layer per drawing per cell-size class;
- **(B3)** one big bindless-style atlas (not available in WebGPU v1).

## Decision

- **Bitmaps: B2.** Each sheet becomes a `texture_2d_array<f32>` of `r8unorm` (ink only; the sheets are grey plus alpha, and only alpha is ink), one layer per cell, with its own full mip chain generated per layer, so no bleed. Strokes are a 60-layer array of 512 × 64 sampled with `repeat` along u. A sprite instance carries its layer index. The fragment shader keeps the reference's smoothstep ink edge and its two threshold pairs.
  - `tools/pack-atlas` converts the sheets once into KTX2 or raw layer files under `assets-built/` (not committed; regenerated by `npm run prepare-assets`). The sources in `assets/` stay untouched.
  - Memory, all sheets with mips: about 5.9 MB (4.4 million texels, r8, plus a third for mips).
- **Vectors: V1.** All vector records are packed into one segment buffer (`vec4<f32>`: a, b) with a per-drawing range table, plus dot and blob buffers. A compute pass (`vector-expand.wgsl`) applies each instance's affine transform and its warp to _densified_ segment end points, then writes one quad per segment, padded by 1 device pixel, whose half-width is the pen weight in plate pixels. The fragment shader computes analytic **capsule coverage**, clamp(w + 0.5 − distance to the segment, 0, 1). That gives round caps and joins and needs no MSAA. In the spike (variant A2) this was the most faithful ribbon at every size: IoU 0.92 / 0.95 / 0.97 against a round-cap Canvas2D stroke at 48, 220 and 1100 px, against 0.89 / 0.90 / 0.90 for the reference's own 0.9w-overlap quads with 4× MSAA. It was also the cheapest. The reference's overlap quads stay available as a parity mode for golden calibration. The whole library is 23,431 segments: 0.37 MB as `vec4<f32>`.
- **No SDF.** The spike (spikes/vector-lines/RESULTS.md) measured SDFs against ribbons on whole/000:
  - **Memory:** the whole library is 375 KB as segments, against 225 MB at 512² r16float or 3.6 GB at 2048².
  - **Pen weight:** a 512² field thins the pen at 1100 px (2.07 px against 2.39; beaded strokes). Only 2048² holds it.
  - **Warps:** non-affine warps need an analytic inverse. The rewind warp has one, but then cost 114 ms against 8 ms for warped ribbons at 1100 px and broke up near the warp centre. The lens and tidal warps have no cheap inverse at all.

  The one result in SDF's favour: 6,000 quads at 48 px drew faster than 6,000 ribbon drawings on SwiftShader (541 ms against 1,236 ms). That is a software vertex pipeline pushing 4 M vertices. If real hardware shows the deep field is vertex-bound, the remedy is a **ribbon level of detail** (decimate polylines for small instances; the mean segment is 1.3 px at 48 px), not a second technique. That is to be measured in M10.

## Consequences

- Pen weight is exact at every size and under every warp, which is the property the brief requires.
- Ribbons cost vertices: about 6 per segment, so at most a few hundred thousand per frame for a full deep field. That is well within budget (spike timings).
- Blobs (filled ellipses) and dots in vector records keep the reference's treatment: knot and dot sprites from the bitmap sheets, chosen by the integer hash.
- Texture arrays limit layers per array (`maxTextureArrayLayers` ≥ 256 by default). The largest sheet, pieces (638 cells), is split into three arrays, or the limit is raised where the adapter allows.
